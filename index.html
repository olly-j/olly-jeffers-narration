02 The work

The work happens before the first word.

A narrator has to understand the writing deeply enough to make the performance feel effortless. The voice should reveal the book, not sit on top of it.

  1. 01

    Prepare the page

    Character, intention, pace and subtext all begin on the page. I prepare for what is said, what is meant and what should remain unspoken.

  2. 02

    Carry the thought

    Years spent working with complex subjects taught me how to carry an argument, protect nuance and bring a listener with me.

  3. 03

    Leave room

    The reading needs life, but it also needs judgement. I look for the performance the book asks for—no more and no less.

03 My story

A return to the work I began with.

I trained as an actor and singer at Drama Centre. Then life took an interesting turn into corporate learning and leadership development.

It was not as far from performance as it sounds. For more than a decade, I stood in front of rooms, made unfamiliar ideas feel manageable and learnt how to hold attention without forcing it.

I kept up voice-over and stage work alongside that career. I am now returning seriously to narration with both parts of my working life intact: the actor’s instinct for character and the communicator’s respect for clarity.
