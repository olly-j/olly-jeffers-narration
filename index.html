The work

The writing comes first.

Good narration begins with understanding what the book is doing. The performance should make that clearer without flattening its character.

  1. 01

    Know where it is going

    Before recording, I work out the shape of the argument, the turns in the story and the moments where the listener may need a little more help.

  2. 02

    Listen for the person

    Every passage has a point of view. I listen for the curiosity, pressure, wit or vulnerability behind it and let that shape the reading.

  3. 03

    Keep the delivery honest

    Clarity matters, but so does restraint. The aim is to keep the listener inside the book rather than draw attention to the narrator.

How I got here

Performance met real life.

I trained as an actor and singer at Drama Centre. My career then moved into corporate learning and leadership development.

For more than a decade, I learnt how to bring people with me through complicated subjects, present to audiences and make unfamiliar ideas feel manageable.

I kept up voice-over and stage work alongside that career. Narration is where the two strands now meet properly. The performance training matters, and so do the years spent making difficult things clear without talking down to people.
