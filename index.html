01 · Approach

Interpret first.
Perform second.

The best narration is not a performance laid on top of a book. It is an interpretation built from inside it.

01

Read for structure

Understand the argument, the narrative turns and the purpose of each passage before deciding how it should sound.

02

Listen for the person

Find the human stakes beneath the information—the curiosity, pressure, wit or vulnerability carrying the words.

03

Serve the sentence

Use clarity, rhythm and restraint so the listener remains inside the writing, not conscious of a narrator performing at them.

02 · The story

An actor’s training.
A communicator’s mileage.

I trained as an actor and singer, including an Associate Diploma in Acting at Drama Centre London. My career then moved into global learning and leadership development.

For more than a decade, I have worked with complex subjects and global audiences—explaining ideas, presenting publicly and helping people find their way into unfamiliar material.

Alongside that career, I continued voice-over and stage work. Narration is where those paths now meet: performance, language, subject fluency and a deep interest in how stories hold attention.

What that means in the booth

  • Comfort with technical language and unfamiliar ideas
  • An actor-trained ear for rhythm, tone and subtext
  • Attention to argument, pacing and narrative shape
  • Range beyond specialist nonfiction

03 · Range

A clear centre.
No artificial ceiling.

Intelligent narrative nonfiction is the clearest point of difference. It is not the edge of the map.

I

Ideas

Technology, AI, psychology, business, learning and popular science—books where understanding the thought is part of telling the story.

II

Lives

Biography, memoir and narrative history, with attention to the person beneath the events and the emotional temperature of the truth.

III

Worlds

Literary and contemporary fiction, speculative fiction and intelligent, character-led thrillers—stories that reward precision and restraint.
